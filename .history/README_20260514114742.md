# SEN201 Inventory and Warehouse Management System

## Project Overview
This project is a Laravel-based web application refined from an earlier business and stock management platform into an academic **Inventory and Warehouse Management System** for a SEN201 Software Engineering group project.

The current implementation is a **server-rendered Laravel monolith** with Blade views, role-based access control, MySQL persistence, and business-aware data separation. It supports warehouse inventory registration, goods receipt, goods dispatch, stock monitoring, low-stock alerts, expense tracking, reporting, and dashboard analytics.

One important implementation detail is that:

- `products` store inventory master data such as item name, category, SKU, unit type, storage location, and threshold settings.
- `purchases` store the current warehouse stock batches used for inventory display and dispatch.
- `purchase_histories` preserve historical receipt records for reporting and edits.

This means the system works as a practical warehouse application while still retaining some broader business-management structures under the hood, especially for super admin oversight, wallet tracking, and credit sales support.

## Inventory Workflow
### System Workflow Summary
The real application flow works like this:

1. A super admin creates a business and the first business admin.
2. The business admin logs in and creates categories and inventory items.
3. Inventory items are defined with SKU, location, unit type, and low-stock threshold.
4. The admin records a goods receipt.
5. The system creates:
   - a live warehouse stock record in `purchases`
   - a historical receipt record in `purchase_histories`
6. Warehouse stock becomes available for dispatch.
7. An admin or salesperson records goods dispatch from available stock.
8. The system creates a `sales` record and reduces the selected `purchases.quantity`.
9. Dashboards and reports update from the new stock and dispatch data.

### Inventory design summary
- `products` are the master inventory register
- `purchases` are the current warehouse stock ledger
- `sales` are outbound stock movements
- `adjustment_products` track manual stock adjustments

## Goods Receipt & Dispatch Process
### Goods Receipt process
Goods receipt is handled by `PurchaseController`.

When an admin records a receipt:

1. The selected product is validated
2. Supplier information, quantity, unit cost, and date are captured
3. A `Purchase` record is created for current warehouse stock
4. A matching `PurchaseHistory` record is created for historical traceability
5. `Product.current_stock` is incremented
6. The business wallet is debited
7. A wallet transaction record is created

The system also supports restocking an existing purchase batch.

### Goods Dispatch process
Goods dispatch is handled by `SalesController`.

When an admin or salesperson records a dispatch:

1. The form loads products directly from available `purchases` with quantity greater than zero
2. The user selects stock batches, quantity, and selling price
3. The system validates stock availability and selling price rules
4. A `Sale` record is created
5. The selected `Purchase.quantity` is reduced
6. The wallet is updated for non-credit sales
7. Creditor balances are updated when the payment type is `credit`

### Important implementation note
The current refined flow no longer depends on `product_assignments` for new goods dispatch records. New dispatches are taken directly from warehouse inventory. Legacy assignment-linked sales are still supported in the codebase for compatibility with older records.

## System Architecture Summary
### Short Architecture Explanation
This application follows a **layered Laravel MVC architecture**:

- **Routes** define entry points and role-based route groups
- **Controllers** coordinate validation, workflow rules, and business operations
- **Models** represent the core entities such as products, purchases, sales, businesses, users, and expenses
- **Blade views** render the user interface with Tailwind CSS styling
- **MySQL** persists the operational and reporting data

The system is also **business-aware**. Instead of using one global tenant scope, many controllers apply filtering through the reusable `BusinessScoped` trait to keep data tied to the currently logged-in business.

### Module Breakdown
- **Authentication Module:** login, password reset, profile management, role redirect
- **Business Module:** business creation, business analytics, business capital, wallet funding
- **Inventory Module:** product register, SKU, category, location, low-stock setup, stock adjustment
- **Category Module:** create, edit, update, and delete categories per business
- **Goods Receipt Module:** supplier-based stock intake with history tracking
- **Goods Dispatch Module:** outbound stock processing from inventory batches
- **Expense Module:** expense entry and daily cost tracking
- **Reporting Module:** sales, profit, inventory, and expense export/reporting
- **Staff Module:** business admin creates and manages staff roles and status
- **Activity Module:** audit logging for model and user activity
- **Support Modules:** documents, creditors, wallets, and legacy assignment support

### Database Summary
The application database is centered around a few core relationships:

- A `business` owns many `users`, `products`, `purchases`, `sales`, `expenses`, `documents`, and `creditors`
- A `category` belongs to a business and has many `products`
- A `product` belongs to a business and optionally belongs to a category
- A `purchase` belongs to a product and represents current stock
- A `sale` belongs to a purchase and represents dispatched stock
- A `wallet` belongs to a business and tracks financial balance changes
- A `creditor` belongs to a business and tracks credit-based customer balances
